Exploration of Exclusive Self Attention

Arya Vohra & Aleksandr Popov

Introduction & Background

  • Exclusive Self Attention (XSA) (Zhai et al., 2026) is a novel modification to the self-attention operation in transformer-based LLMs. For each attention head, it orthogonalizes the attention outputs relative to the current token's value vector, removing the component along the self-value direction.
  • For head $h$ at position $i$, standard attention is modified by XSA to project the attention output $y_{h,i}$ onto the orthogonal complement of the self-value vector $v_{h,i}$:
Standard Attention $\displaystyle y_{h,i}=\sum_{j\leq i} a_{h,ij}v_{h,j}$
Exclusive Self Attention (XSA) $\displaystyle z_{h,i} = \left( I - \frac{v_{h,i}v_{h,i}^{\top}}{\|v_{h,i}\|^2} \right) y_{h,i}$
  • Core Intuition: Attention should prioritize modeling contextual information from other tokens, since information about the current token is already natively preserved through the residual stream and subsequent feedforward networks (FFN).
  • XSA also acts as an implicit attention sink. By removing the attention component along $v_i$, models can discard attention mass by attending heavily to the self-value, allowing tokens to "attend to nothing" without requiring an explicit sink token.

Existence of the Phenomenon

  • To confirm the underlying premise of XSA, we trained an 11-layer GPT-2-style model on FineWeb and analyzed the alignment between attention outputs $y_i$ and self-values $v_i$.
  • We observe consistent, strong $v_i$-alignment across heads and layers, demonstrating that standard attention wastes capacity on self-token information.

Training an XSA Model

  • We explored three distinct structural variations of implementing the XSA orthogonalization principle across different spaces of the Transformer:
  • Value Space (Standard): Projects $y_{h,i}$ against $v_{h,i}$ on a per-head basis. It is the default implementation, performing well but suffering from slight orthogonalization overhead.
  • Residual Space: Projects the post-$W_o$ final attention output $o_i$ directly against the residual token representation $x_i$:
    $$o_i'= o_i-\frac{o_i^\top x_i}{\|x_i\|^2}x_i$$
  • Model Space (Diag): Projects $o_i$ against the combined subspace span of all head self-values in the model space. Let $u_{h,i}=W_o^{(h)}v_{h,i}$, $U_i=\operatorname{orth}(u_{1,i},\ldots,u_{H,i})$, and:
    $$o_i'=(I- U_iU_i^\top)o_i$$

  • Sinking Hypothesis: Does the model learn that self-attention components are actively discarded? We observed that XSA models allocate significantly higher attention mass towards the self-token, validating that it acts as an implicit, low-overhead sink.

Improving XSA in NanoGPT Speedrun

  • The record Modded NanoGPT implementation contains a quasi-attention sink called "Sparse Attention Gates".
  • Since we demonstrated that XSA naturally behaves as a robust attention sink, we hypothesized that the Sparse Attention Gates would become completely redundant.
  • Removing these gates and replacing them with XSA resulted in a cleaner architecture that achieves the validation loss target faster.

Conclusions & Future Work

  • Valuable Inductive Bias: Confirms the foundational intuition of self-attention as a purely contextual mixing operator. Forcing attention outputs to be orthogonal to the self-value improves general learning efficiency.
  • Implicit Sinks Work: Confirms that XSA provides a robust, zero-parameter alternative to explicit attention sinks, naturally absorbing redundant attention mass on diagonal indices.
  • Nuanced Scaling Required: XSA is not a blind drop-in. Layer-selective gating (e.g., lower strengths in initial layers) is key to optimal convergence in deeply stacked architectures.
